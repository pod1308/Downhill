The Bay is the bottom of the funnel.

Rain that lands on a street in Oakland does not stay there. It runs to a gutter, into a storm drain, out to a creek, and into the estuary, usually within hours and usually untreated. The blue lines on this map are the real drainage network, drawn from OpenStreetMap hydrography.

02

Industry settled on the flats.

Refineries, treatment plants and cleanup sites line the East Bay and South Bay shoreline, where runoff has the shortest path to open water. Flat land near water was cheap, and it was zoned for the uses nobody wanted next door. Every facility here is a real, publicly documented site.

Facilities: public regulatory records i
03

The burden is measured, and it is uneven.

California scores every census tract for cumulative environmental burden and ranks it against the rest of the state. This map draws those official scores directly from the state's own service. The highest scoring Bay Area tracts cluster along the industrial shoreline and the freeway corridors.

Scores: CalEnviroScreen, OEHHA i
04

Nearness is a question, not a verdict.

A facility sitting in a high scoring tract is a fact about geography. It is not proof that the facility caused the score. CalEnviroScreen combines around twenty indicators, most unrelated to any single site. This atlas is built to help you ask a better question, then check the answer against the regulatory record.
